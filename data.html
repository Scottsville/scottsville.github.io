The second plant — same promise, 2025

Tenaska's proposed Expedition Generating Station — a 1,500 MW plant adjacent to the existing one — projects 1,000+ construction jobs over four years and 29 permanent employees once complete. The Fluvanna County community stated it had not seen the benefits originally promised when the first plant was approved. The Planning Commission recommended denial. The Board of Supervisors approved it 4–1.

Sources: Tenaska Virginia Partners LP; Expedition Generating Station FAQs; Fluvanna Review (Aug. 2025); Cville Right Now (Jan. 2026); Piedmont Environmental Council.

The income & poverty gap

Scottsville is part of Albemarle County — one of Virginia's wealthiest. The Tenaska plant sits just across in Fluvanna County, so its tax revenue goes there. The residents who live next to it, breathe its emissions, and hear its noise are Albemarle County residents. They benefit from neither county's share of the plant's revenue.

Contributors & extractors

A contributor leaves more in a place than it takes out. An extractor does the reverse. The distinction is not ideological — it is a ledger.

The pattern

Each entity that arrived with a promise — jobs, growth, prosperity — extracted payroll, tax benefits, or economic activity from the community and relocated it elsewhere. The physical infrastructure stayed. The economic benefit left. The entities that contribute most are the ones that were never offered tax abatements or approval hearings: the families, the churches, the volunteer fire department, the neighbors who show up.

Sources: US Census Bureau; Tenaska Virginia Partners LP; Fluvanna Review; Cvillepedia; C-VILLE Weekly; Piedmont Environmental Council. Ledger entries based on documented public record.
